# August 8, 2026 capacity measurements

The phase 2 capacity record used `free -g`, `pvesm status`, and each guest's `pct config` on both hypervisors before declaring additional guests.

Measured capacity:

| Hypervisor | Physical memory | Configured guest memory | Free datastore space |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vault | 94 GB | 58.8 GB, or 60,160 MB across nine guests | 390 GB on local-lvm |
| suburban | 31 GB | 13.4 GB, or 13,696 MB across seven guests | 1,140 GB on local-zfs |

Guest 117 used a 24,576 MB memory allocation, and guest 217 used 8,192 MB. Each guest still ran every data service at the time of the record.

## Earlier sizing proposal

The initial proposal assigned 16,384 MB to each production data guest and 6,144 MB to each testbed data guest. Its additions required 57,344 MB on vault and 22,528 MB on suburban. The resulting vault allocation would total about 115 GB against 94 GB of physical memory. The proposed 360 GB of disk would leave 30 GB free. Suburban had only 17.6 GB of memory headroom.

## Revised sizing decision

The record separated testbed and production sizing.

Revised guest sizes:

| Environment | Guests | Cores per guest | Memory per guest | Disk per guest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Testbed on suburban | Three data guests | 2 | 4,096 MB | 40 GB |
| Testbed on suburban | Second app guest | 2 | 2,048 MB | 30 GB |
| Production on vault | Three data guests | 4 | 16,384 MB | 60 GB |
| Production on vault | Second app guest | 4 | 8,192 MB | 40 GB |

The record reported the testbed sizing as applied. Its 14,336 MB addition left about 3 GB of headroom. The testbed could validate topology, replication, failover, and export shape. It could not establish memory headroom for three production-sized database nodes. The plan required proportionally smaller testbed engine limits and separate production values.

Production sizing still required operator approval. The revised 220 GB disk addition would leave 170 GB free. The memory calculation assumed reclamation of the unmanaged debianct sandbox, VMID 100, with eight cores and 16,384 MB, after confirmation. It also assumed that guest 117 would shrink as data services moved to the new guests. Both changes would produce about 79 GB of configured guest memory against 94 GB of physical memory. The record did not authorize sandbox deletion or prove that either change occurred.

The [original capacity record](https://github.com/agoodkind/tack/blob/7e469f9851c55e0ae36a03fe279287c6a430da05/docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-08-phase2-capacity.md) distinguished empty guests from guests running database services. Memory ceilings did not reserve memory, but starting the databases would make the overcommit relevant. These historical readings do not establish current available capacity.
